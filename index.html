Embody

An Isaac-native policy training toolkit.

Connecting robot definitions, simulation, data, and learning in one training workflow.

Personal project · Prototype

Tools for robots
that learn.

Embody helps define robots, scenes, tasks, observations, and actions—then connects data collection, training, policy artifacts, and evaluation.

I’m building a coherent path from a robotics task to a policy you can inspect and evaluate. Deployed policies still need application-specific calibration, observations, and control loops.

Inside the toolkit

Project-owned tasks and dependency environments keep each training project self-contained. Typed references resolve tasks and robots; versioned artifacts track datasets, checkpoints, policies, and evaluations. Dry, fast, and vision profiles support resource checks, Isaac execution without camera rendering, and camera-based review.

Embody is an evolving prototype. APIs, storage layouts, policy interfaces, and CLI commands may change as the design develops.

Sim-to-real for contact-rich manipulation

May 2025 – June 2026 · Wearable robotics & AI control

Sensor integration & exoskeleton assistance

My wearable robotics research connected human intent to physical assistance. I developed an EMG-to-assist control pipeline for the Kenqing hip exoskeleton, mapping surface EMG signals and IMU gait features to real-time, phase-synchronized torque actuation.

Reliable sensing started at the hardware. I designed and 3D-printed a modular knee mount in SolidWorks for repeatable IMU and EMG placement, and supported participant data collection for robot policy training. I also constructed subject-specific musculoskeletal digital twins in MATLAB to simulate biological hip moments and establish assistance targets around the stance-to-swing transition.

Alongside sensing and control, I helped diagnose robot power failures with multimeters and oscilloscopes, isolate fault locations, and replace components to maintain the research platform.
